# Chapter 4 part 2

The rest of chapter 4 further explores further ways to guide user actions.

Forcing functions are constraints that are more "forceful.

  - Interlocks are operations that prevent eachother, like a dead man's switch, having to hold a button to operate a machine.
  - Lock-ins stop you from leaving, like a warning message that asks if you're sure if you want to exit without saving
  - Lock-outs, opposite of lock-ins prevent you from doing something potentially high-risk

Conventions are also explored more, how culturally learned standards can guide behaviors, and how designers have to consider and work around them. He uses examples of red/green light signals and measurement systems. This reminds me of Japanese use of "kawaii" or cute things in their signage, being culturally acceptable, or even expected, when a topic is serious.
